# EV read — the model does not make money where copybot trades

2026-10-19. `scripts/ml-ev.py`, `.venv-ml`, accounting reused verbatim from the repo:
`feePerShare = rate · price · (1−price)` (`src/lib/scoring/price-edge.ts`), rates 0.07/0.04/0.05
by category (`categoryFeeRate`), `shares = stake / price` (`src/lib/training-export.ts`).

---

## 0. The answer in one paragraph

Copying everything **loses 3.9 %** (CI excludes zero). The model is a real ranker (AUC 0.737) but
its high-confidence picks are **expensive favourites that win 77 % of the time and still lose
money** — ranking by probability is not ranking by edge. The profitable region of the book is
**cheap longshots** (cost ≤ 0.20), where a *blind* rule returns **+36.8 %** — and **inside that
region the model earns its keep**, splitting it into **+106.8 %** and **−32.3 %** halves with both
CIs excluding zero. Everywhere else it is useless (mid-range AUC 0.536). Copybot, meanwhile, is
selecting the wrong side of this boundary: it acted on **54** of the 473 cheap rows (and lost 4.8 %
on them) and declined the **419** that return +42.2 %.

## 1. Whole book — the base case

| | n | hit | mean cost | ROI | CI95 (market-clustered) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| take everything | 13,770 | 0.600 | 0.634 | **−0.0392** | [−0.0586, −0.0201] |

Cost > hit rate is the whole story: the average copy pays 63.4 cents for a 60.0 % shot, and the
taker fee makes it worse. **No model fixes this by ranking better** — the price is the problem.

## 2. Why "good AUC" does not save it

Splitting the whole book by model score:

| selection | n | hit | ROI |
|---|---|---|---|
| model **top half** | 6,885 | **0.770** | **−0.0222** |
| model bottom half | 6,885 | 0.429 | −0.0562 |
| **price-only** top half | 6,872 | 0.743 | **+0.0513** |

The model's top half wins **77 %** of its bets and still **loses money**. It is confidently selecting
expensive favourites — exactly the bets the market already overprices (see §4). A pure price model
does *better* economically while ranking worse. **AUC is the wrong objective for this decision.**

## 3. Where the money actually is, and whether the model adds anything

**Cheap subset (cost ≤ 0.20), fees included, market-clustered bootstrap (600 resamples):**

| selection | n | hit | ROI | CI95 | P(ROI>0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| take all cheap rows | 473 | 0.230 | +0.368 | [+0.089, +0.629] | 0.995 |
| **model top half** | 235 | **0.340** | **+1.068** | **[+0.665, +1.486]** | **1.000** |
| model bottom half | 238 | 0.122 | **−0.323** | [−0.573, −0.068] | 0.007 |
| price-only top half | 232 | 0.302 | +0.777 | [+0.444, +1.162] | 1.000 |

Inside the cheap region the model is genuinely additive: **+106.8 % vs +77.7 %** for the price-only
arm, and it correctly identifies a **−32.3 %** half to avoid. AUC within the subset is **0.699
(model) vs 0.621 (price-only)**.

**Mid-priced rows (0.35–0.55) — where copybot actually trades:**

| | n | hit | ROI | AUC (best model) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mid price | 3,344 | 0.465 | **−0.0136** | **0.536** (≈ chance) |

Mid-priced bets price fairly (ROI −1.4 %, CI straddles zero — this is the sanity check that says the
cost model is sound) and **no model can rank them**.

## 4. The structural fact underneath: longshot–favourite bias

Realised hit rate by cost decile on the test book:

| cost band | realised hit | edge (hit − cost) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.11–0.33 | 0.289 | **+0.052** |
| 0.33–0.44 | 0.458 | **+0.062** |
| 0.44–0.51 | 0.462 | −0.019 |
| 0.64–0.72 | 0.604 | −0.080 |
| 0.85–0.90 | 0.854 | −0.027 |
| 0.90–0.99 | 0.865 | **−0.095** |

Cheap tokens win **more** than they cost; expensive tokens win **less**. Copybot buys expensive.

## 5. What copybot is doing with this

| | n | ROI |
|---|---|---|
| cheap rows copybot **acted** on | 54 | **−0.048** |
| cheap rows copybot **declined** | 419 | **+0.422** [+0.155, +0.709] |
| copybot's whole acted selection | 1,291 | **−0.081** [−0.148, −0.015] |

On the same accounting, copybot's own live selection loses money, it is on the wrong side of the
cheap/expensive boundary, and the trades it declines are the profitable ones.

## 6. What this does NOT authorize

- **n = 235** in the decisive cell. The CI is [+0.665, +1.486] — a factor of two, not a tight estimate.
- **Fillability is unmodelled and is the biggest risk.** These are longshots averaging 0.16 with thin
  books; the ask we used may not be available in size. A +107 % paper ROI on illiquid longshots is
  the most fragile kind of result and nothing in this data can settle it.
- **Two months, one holdout, one wallet set.** August alone gave +0.047 (CI including zero); all of
  the signal is September onward.
- The bias may be **these wallets' skill**, not a market inefficiency. Untested.
- Fees are modelled at the repo's category rates; if the true rate differs the whole table moves.

## 7. What it does authorize

**Stop training the model to predict outcomes across the whole book. It is a ranker for the cheap
subset.** Concretely, the next read is a pre-registered admission rule on the region that pays:

> cost ≤ 0.20 **and** `p_model − cost − fee > threshold`, sized small, with the fill assumption
> stated and tested against book depth before any live change.

And a separate, larger finding that has nothing to do with ML: **copybot's selection currently
favours the expensive side of a book where expensive bets lose.** That deserves its own
investigation before any model goes live, because a better ranker on top of the wrong region
changes nothing.
